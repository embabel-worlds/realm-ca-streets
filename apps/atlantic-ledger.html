The Atlantic Ledger ■■

Britain and Canada, measured against each other from their own official registers — eleven UK sources and nine Canadian ones sharing one graph, so every panel below is a single query that crosses the ocean. Every cross-country number is dimensionless; every figure names the register it came from, because the registers are cousins, not twins.

The affordability ladder

Years of local income to buy the local home, at every watched place in both countries — one dimensionless ladder. The UK divides by an individual's pay, Canada by a household's, so a UK rung carries a structural handicap of roughly the second earner: gaps of a year or two are noise, gaps of five are geography.

Whose crime map is more unequal?

Each country's crime figures reduced to a dimensionless spread. The metrics differ — a per-1,000 rate against a severity index — so only the spread columns compare, never the means. Britain is measured at ~300 districts and Canada at 41 metros, which mechanically widens the UK spread; the direction survives the caveat.

Do the same things predict crime?

Three gradients computed for both countries in one query — a cross-national replication test. A faded bar is an association the sample cannot distinguish from zero. What replicates is structure; what appears in one country only is circumstance.

What best predicts crime, here and there

Each country's own six-predictor model, standardized betas so the bars are comparable within each column. Britain's crime is explained by what a place is — deprivation, density, age. Canada's structural variables carry little; the clearance rate and region carry the map.

Impunity, both sides

The share of reported crime where nobody is held to account. The instruments differ — the UK figure is one month of street incidents already closed, open cases excluded; the Canadian is a full-year severity-weighted national mean — so read each row with its metric, never as one league table.

What only Britain can see

How few streets carry half of each watched UK place's crime. Canada publishes no street-level register at all, so this measurement is structurally impossible there — which is itself an Atlantic finding: Britain can see crime concentration and Canada cannot.

Association across areas, never causation — and never a statement about any person. How this works

One graph, two countries

Both realms — ca-streets and realm-uk-streets — install into one knowledge graph with one global label space, so a single saved view can traverse (u:UkDistricts)-[:LISTS_CRIME]→ and (ca:CaCmaSet)-[:LISTS_CSI_NOW]→ in the same statement. Nothing here is stored or synced: every figure is fetched from its national source when the view runs, cached at that source's cadence.

The views this page runs

  • AtlanticAffordability — dwelling value over household income (Census 2021, per watched Canadian place) beside average sale price over median full-time pay (HM Land Registry HPI and ONS ASHE, per watched UK place).
  • AtlanticCrimeGeography — each country's crime figures reduced to count, mean, coefficient of variation and extremes. City of London is excluded (suppressed cell); Westminster's rate is daytime-population-inflated, so the CV is the headline, never max/min.
  • AtlanticGradients — income, young-adult share and population each correlated with crime in both countries, in one query, with confidence intervals and detectability.
  • WhatBestPredictsCrime (UK) and WhatBestPredictsCanadianCrime — each country's six-predictor least-squares fit, standardized betas, honest R².
  • AtlanticImpunity — Canada's severity-weighted share of crime not cleared, beside each watched UK place's share of last month's street crimes closed with no suspect.
  • WhereCrimeConcentrates — the Pareto count of streets carrying half of each UK place's crime, from data.police.uk street-level incidents.

What an empty panel means

An empty affordability, impunity or concentration panel usually means no places are being watched in that country — those three ride watched places; the others ride national anchors and need none. A panel that names a source failure is a connectivity problem at the register, not an answer. The UK column of any panel goes empty if realm-uk-streets is not installed beside this realm.

Reading it honestly

  • Every cross-country number here is dimensionless — a ratio, a share, a coefficient. Raw pounds never sit beside raw dollars.
  • UK crime counts offences where they happen; Canadian severity weights them by seriousness and is police-reported. Both inflate or deflate places for reasons that are about measurement, and the basis lines say so.
  • Britain's ~300 districts detect gradients Canada's 41 metros cannot — an undetectable Canadian bar beside a detectable UK one of similar size is a power difference, not a contradiction.

What the appliance did here that a conventional stack could not

Twenty national registers, two countries, no ETL, no warehouse, no sync jobs. Each panel is one saved view; the join across the Atlantic is a property of the graph, not a pipeline. The conventional path to this page is two data teams and a reconciliation project — and its numbers would be as old as the last run.
