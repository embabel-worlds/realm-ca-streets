What predicts crime in Canada?

Five Statistics Canada registers, joined on the census metropolitan area, over the same ~39 CMAs. Tick and untick predictors below and watch what each one does to the others — a variable's apparent strength usually belongs to the variables it travels with, and no single model can show you that. Leaving unemployment on its own, then adding the clearance rate and income back, is the shortest route to the point.

The model

Tick a predictor to put it in the fit. Alone is what that variable claims on its own; in model is what survives beside the others you have enabled. They are always shown together, because the gap between them is the whole point.

Reading the numbers. Each runs from −1 to +1 and says how strongly a CMA's crime severity moves with that variable, in like-for-like units, so the six are directly comparable. 0 is no association at all; + means more of it goes with more severity, − means more of it goes with less. Roughly: under 0.15 is very weak, 0.3 moderate, above 0.5 strong. With only ~39 CMAs in the country, a weak association is usually indistinguishable from nothing — the "alone" column says when that is so.

The data, and where each number comes from

Five Statistics Canada registers that do not meet on any StatCan page, joined on the census metropolitan area. Nothing here is stored: every figure is fetched from the source when you open the page, and the joins are made at query time.

  • Crime — the Crime Severity Index by CMA, latest published year (table 35-10-0026). A national index, Canada 2006 = 100, seriousness-weighted, so areas rank directly. The outcome is the level, and it is police-reported throughout.
  • Income — median total income of census families, T1FF 2023 (table 11-10-0009).
  • Unemployment — the LFS unemployment rate by CMA, three-month moving average, seasonally adjusted, June 2026 (table 14-10-0459).
  • Age — the share of residents aged 20–24, July 1 2025 demographic estimates (table 17-10-0148) — criminology's age-crime curve axis. The 20–24 count and the population it is divided by come from the same cube and the same vintage.
  • Growth — population change 2021→2025 from the same estimates.
  • Clearance — the weighted clearance rate, the share of reported crime solved, seriousness-weighted, latest year (table 35-10-0026). This is a policing variable, not a structural one — see the caveat below.

The view that runs

One saved view does the work, and the engine computes every coefficient — the page does no arithmetic:

CaCrimeModelWithPredictors(income, unemployment, youngAdults, growth, clearance, population)

MATCH (u:CaCmaSet {set:'canada'})-[:LISTS_CSI_NOW]->(n:CaCmaCsiNow)
/* each national one-call series maps its WDS vector to the CMA name … */
MATCH (u)-[:LISTS_INCOME]->(inc:CaCmaIncome)         /* T1FF 2023 */
MATCH (u)-[:LISTS_UNEMPLOYMENT]->(j:CaCmaUnemployment) /* LFS mid-2026 */
MATCH (u)-[:LISTS_YOUTH_POP]->(yp:CaCmaYouthPop)     /* aged 20-24, 2025 */
MATCH (u)-[:LISTS_POP_2021]->(pa) MATCH (u)-[:LISTS_POP_2025]->(pb)
MATCH (u)-[:LISTS_CLEAR_NOW]->(cn:CaCmaClearNow)
WHERE /* every predictor non-null, enabled or not — see below */
WITH cma, csi, [x IN [
       CASE WHEN $income       THEN income END,
       CASE WHEN $unemployment THEN unemployment END,
       CASE WHEN $youngAdults  THEN youngAdults END,
       CASE WHEN $growth       THEN growth END,
       CASE WHEN $clearance    THEN clearance END,
       CASE WHEN $population   THEN population END
     ] WHERE x IS NOT NULL] AS predictors
RETURN regress(predictors, csi, cma) AS model

Why the sample never changes

Every predictor is required non-null whether or not you have enabled it, so the same CMAs are in every fit. Without that the sample would move as you toggle, and a coefficient that changed would leave you unable to tell whether the model changed or the data did. That is the exact confusion this page exists to remove. Two areas are absent by the sources' own shape: Oshawa has no published severity vector, and Ottawa–Gatineau is policed as two separate parts while its predictors describe the combined CMA, so it cannot be joined honestly.

What the numbers are

Every figure on the page is a standardized coefficient. Each predictor is expressed in standard deviations rather than its own units — dollars, percentage points, people — so that income and unemployment and a count can be compared on one scale without one of them looking larger merely for being measured in bigger numbers. A coefficient of 0.5 means: a CMA one standard deviation above average on that predictor sits about half a standard deviation above average on crime severity, holding every other enabled predictor still. R² is the share of the variation between CMAs that the enabled predictors account for together.

What "alone" means

The alone figure is the same cross-section with exactly one variable correlated against severity. A standardized beta from a single-predictor fit is that variable's correlation, so both columns come from one method — no second measure, nothing to drift.

The clearance caveat

Clearance is in the battery because Canada publishes it and it is the strongest crime signal this realm has found — but its direction is genuinely ambiguous on its face. Solving crime may suppress it; a force swamped by rising crime solves less of it. Read its coefficient as the impunity association, never as an effect of policing. The other five are structural and come from registers the police never touch. Two saved views take the question further than this page can: WhatForetoldCanadianCrime refits the whole model on previous-period vintages (2018 income, June-2019 unemployment, 2021 age, growth and clearance), so every predictor precedes the 2025 outcome; ClearanceLeadsOrLags runs the cross-lagged test — does an early clearance change precede the later severity change, or the reverse?

What an empty panel means

If the model panel is empty, the fit could not be computed — most likely a source did not answer (Statistics Canada throttles hard by IP), in which case the page says so rather than showing you a zero. A blank residual list with a live model means no CMA sits far enough from prediction to name, which is not a failure. Zero predictors enabled is refused by the page, because a model with nothing in it is not a model.

What the appliance did here that a conventional stack could not

Five cubes, one join key, no ETL and no warehouse. Nothing was copied, scheduled or kept in sync: the page asks a question and the appliance fetches each national series in one cached call, joins them in the query, runs a least-squares fit inside the query, and returns coefficients. Re-enabling a predictor re-fits over live data in about a second. The conventional path to this page is a pipeline per cube, a schema to hold them, a job to refresh them, and a notebook to model them — and the answer would be as old as the last run.

Reading it honestly

  • Association across areas, never causation, and never a claim about any person.
  • Police-reported severity throughout: reporting propensity differs by place, and a CMA can rank high by reporting more rather than suffering more.
  • ~39 areas is a small cross-section: only strong associations are statistically detectable, and one nominally-detectable correlation in six can arrive by chance.
  • R² is modest by design. The joinable open data holds economic and structural variables, not the policing, reporting and opportunity factors that drive much of the remainder.
